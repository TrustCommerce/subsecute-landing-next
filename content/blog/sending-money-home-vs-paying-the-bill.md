Both are normal. Both are fine. They just solve different problems, and most people use one method for everything because it is the one they set up first.

## When sending money is better

**When you do not know what they need.** Cash is flexible. A bill payment is not.

**When it is not about bills at all.** Food, transport, an emergency, school supplies. None of that has a biller code.

**When the person on the other side is the one managing things.** If your mother knows exactly what needs paying and when, sending her money and staying out of the way is the respectful option as well as the efficient one.

**When the amount changes constantly.** Fixed setups do not suit variable needs.

## When paying the bill directly is better

**When it is the same bill every month.** Rent, cable, electricity, school fees, an internet plan.

**When missing it has consequences.** Disconnection, late fees, a child sent home.

**When you want a record.** A receipt or a token is proof. A transfer plus a verbal confirmation is not.

**When you do not want the money to have to be managed at all.** For a lot of families this is the quiet reason. Nobody says it out loud, and that is fine.

## Most people need both

The honest answer is that you probably want a monthly transfer for general support and a fixed setup for the bills that repeat.

Splitting them makes both easier. The recurring bills stop being something anyone thinks about. The money you send is genuinely free for whatever comes up, instead of being partly spoken for before it arrives.

Subsecute handles the recurring half. The bills that repeat get paid on schedule, with a reminder before each one, so the transfers you send can be for everything else.
