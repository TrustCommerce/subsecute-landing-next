The obvious way to build this app is to save your card and charge it on the renewal date. That is what most services do, and it is one less step for you.

We do it the other way round. You top up your Subsecute balance, and renewals come out of that balance.

Here is the reasoning, since it is a fair thing to ask about.

## Charging a card on a fixed date is fragile

A renewal date is a single moment. If anything is wrong at that moment, the payment fails.

Not enough in the account that morning. The card was replaced last week. A limit changed. The bank is having a bad day. None of these are your fault, and all of them cost you the subscription.

Funding ahead moves the risky part to a time that suits you. If a top-up fails on Tuesday, you have until the renewal to sort it. If a charge fails on renewal day, you find out afterwards.

To be clear about what we are and are not saying: we do not control your bank or the card networks, and we cannot promise a payment will never fail. What we can do is stop the whole thing from depending on one transaction landing at one exact minute.

## You keep the decision

Because the money is already in your balance, we can tell you before it moves.

A reminder comes ahead of each renewal. If you still want the subscription, it continues. If you do not, you stop it before the debit, not by chasing a refund after.

An app that has your card on file has no reason to ask you first. An app that pays from your balance does.

## What it costs you

One extra step. You have to remember to keep a balance.

We think it is a fair trade for knowing what is going out before it goes, and for renewals that do not quietly fail. If you disagree, tell us. That kind of feedback is worth more to us right now than another thousand signups.

Set. Automate. Relax.
