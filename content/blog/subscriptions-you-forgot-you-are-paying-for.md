Some subscriptions announce themselves. You watch Netflix, you know you pay for Netflix.

The rest just sit there.

## The usual suspects

**Cloud storage.** You paid for the upgrade once when your phone said it was full. That was two phones ago.

**A design or editing tool.** Signed up for one job. The job ended. The subscription did not.

**A free trial that converted.** You meant to cancel on day six. Day seven happened.

**A course or learning platform.** Bought in a burst of ambition in January.

**An AI tool you were testing.** You have three. You use one.

**A VPN.** For that one thing you needed it for, in 2023.

**A game pass.** For a game you finished.

**Something on your Apple or Google account.** These are the hardest to spot because they show as one charge from Apple or Google, not from the app itself.

## What ₦2,900 a month looks like after two years

Imagine signing up for a design tool in January 2024 for one job. A logo, a few flyers, done in three weeks. ₦2,900 a month.

The job ends in February. The subscription does not. It is a small enough charge that it never stands out in a bank alert, and it has a name that looks like any other card transaction.

By August 2026 that is around ₦90,000, for three weeks of work finished two and a half years ago. Nothing went wrong. Nobody made a mistake. The charge just never had a reason to stop.

## The ten-minute audit

1. Open your bank app and go back three months.
2. Search for any name that appears in all three months.
3. Write each one down with the amount.
4. Check your Apple and Google subscription pages separately, because those charges group together and hide the details.
5. Anything you have not opened in a month, cancel now while it is in front of you.

Do not save it for later. Later is how you got here.

## What to do afterwards

The audit works once. Two months later, three new things have quietly started and you are back to guessing.
