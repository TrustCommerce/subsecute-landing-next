Grey is a good product. If you are reading this expecting us to say otherwise, you can stop here.

It is also built for a different job than Subsecute, and the difference matters most on the one thing this blog is about: a subscription that has to renew every month without you thinking about it.

## What Grey costs

From [Grey's own fees page](https://grey.co/blog/fees-and-charges-on-grey), accurate as of March 2026:

- **$5.00** to create a virtual card, once
- **$1.00** every time you load the card
- **2% + $0.50** on a cross-border transaction, meaning the merchant charges in a currency your card is not in
- **$0.00** monthly maintenance, and no fee to open a Grey account
- **₦35** flat to withdraw to a Nigerian bank account, instantly
- **0.8%** to deposit USD by ACH or FedNow, minimum $2, capped at $10
- Conversion sits between **0% and 1%** depending on the currency pair, capped at $6 on the 1% tier

Those numbers are six months old at the time of writing. Check the page before you rely on them.

## What Grey is genuinely better at

**Receiving money from abroad.** Grey gives you foreign account details and charges 0.8% to deposit, capped at $10. Subsecute does not do this at all. If you freelance and get paid in dollars, that is the whole reason to use Grey and nothing here changes it.

**Getting dollars into naira.** ₦35 flat to withdraw is cheap, and it is instant. Again, not something Subsecute does.

**Knowing the price up front.** Grey publishes every fee on one page. We do not have an equivalent page. Subsecute shows you the fee, the exchange rate and the total on the screen where you confirm the transaction, before the money moves, but you cannot look it up in advance the way you can with Grey. That is a fair point against us.

**One card for everything.** Grey gives you a card and it works on Apple Pay and Google Pay in supported regions. Subsecute issues a separate card per subscription, which is useful for cancelling one without touching the rest, and no use at all if what you wanted was a single card.

## What happens to a subscription on each

This is the part that is actually different, and it is worth quoting Grey directly rather than characterising them.

Grey's [guidance on its virtual cards](https://grey.co/blog/virtual-cards-updates), published in August 2023, tells you to top up the card before the due date for subscriptions like Spotify and Apple Music. It then says what happens if you do not. The card freezes after, in Grey's words, "two(2) initiated transactions with insufficient funds", and after a total of "four (4) failed transactions" from insufficient funds it is terminated permanently.

That page is three years old, so check it still holds before you rely on it. But the shape of the thing is what matters, and it describes the normal failure mode for a Nigerian paying a foreign subscription. You forget to top up. The charge fails. You forget again. Two more and the card is gone, and you are creating a new one at $5 and re-entering it everywhere.

None of that is Grey being careless. It is what a prepaid card has to do. It is just that the work of remembering the date sits with you.

## What Subsecute does instead

The date is the product, not something you hold in your head.

- **You get told first.** A reminder goes out before each renewal on WhatsApp, with the amount and the date, and two buttons: keep it, or cancel the subscription.
- **Nothing moves until you answer.** If you ignore it, nothing is charged. The subscription may lapse with the merchant, which is a real consequence, but it is the one you chose.
- **We charge your saved card before the date, not on it.** We take the money from the card or direct debit you saved a few days ahead, convert at the rate shown to you, and put it on that subscription's card. By the time the merchant asks, the money is already there.
- **One card per subscription.** Cancelling Netflix does not touch Spotify, and a problem with one merchant cannot reach the rest.
- **Somebody else can pay.** Share a link and let a family member fund a specific bill, or run a plan for up to 25 people on your balance with a monthly limit each.

## What Subsecute does not do

Being honest about it, because the list above only means something next to this one.

Subsecute does not give you foreign account details to receive money into. Funding comes from a Nigerian card or bank transfer, so it is not a dollar account and it does not replace your bank. It is not where you save or invest either.

If what you need is somewhere for dollars to land, this is the wrong app and Grey is a reasonable answer.

## Which one to pick

**Pick Grey** if you are paid in dollars, if you want published fees you can check before signing up, or if you want one card you spend with generally.

**Pick Subsecute** if the recurring charges are the thing going wrong. If you have lost a subscription to a failed renewal, if you are covering bills for someone else, or if you would rather answer one message a month than remember five dates.

**Use both** if you want. They are not really competing for the same job. A lot of people receive with one and automate with the other.
