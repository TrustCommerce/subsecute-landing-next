Quick test. Without checking your bank app, name every subscription you are paying for.

Netflix. Spotify. YouTube Premium. Maybe DSTV or GOtv. Prime Video. A cloud storage plan you bought when your phone was full. Canva Pro from one design job. Some AI tool you were "just testing".

Most people can name four or five. Most people are paying for about eight.

The three you forgot are still charging you. That is how ₦3,000 a month becomes ₦36,000 a year for something you last opened in March.

## It is not that you are careless

Subscriptions are easy to start and annoying to keep track of. That is by design.

Signing up takes thirty seconds and one card. Cancelling means you have to remember the thing exists, remember which email you used, find the settings page, then click through two or three screens built to talk you out of it. Nobody does that on a Tuesday evening.

Then there is the Nigerian part:

- Your naira card gets declined on a foreign service and the subscription just dies
- You lose your progress, then resubscribe later at a higher price
- Renewal dates are spread all over the month and there is nowhere to see them together
- Prices go up quietly and the debit just gets bigger
- You pay for the family plan and spend the rest of the month chasing people for their share

So you are running about a dozen recurring payments using your memory and your bank alerts. It was never going to work.

## What automating it actually does

![A monthly bill illustration wrapped in app logos, under the line "Your bills shouldn't need your attention every month."](/images/blog/bills-on-autopilot.webp)

"Automate your subscriptions" sounds like it means spend more without thinking. It does the opposite. Once you can see everything, you start cutting.

**Everything in one place.** All your subscriptions, dates and amounts on one screen. The moment you see it laid out, you will cancel two or three things immediately. Everyone does.

**No renewal happens quietly.** You get told before money moves. Reminder comes in, you confirm or you cancel. You do not have to remember the date, but you still make the call.

**Renewal day stops depending on your card.** Naira cards and foreign services have a rough relationship. Declines, sudden limits, banks changing rules. If your money is already sitting in your wallet before the renewal date, your card is not being tested at the exact moment the subscription is due. And if a top-up fails, you have days to fix it instead of finding out after the fact.

**Shared plans collect themselves.** If you pay for the family Netflix, you already know "I will send you my share" is a promise, not a payment. When the split is set up, everyone's part comes in before the bill is due. You stop being the group accountant.

**Bills back home stop being a whole process.** If you live abroad, sorting a bill for family in Nigeria usually means checking a rate, moving money, sending instructions, then following up twice. It should just happen on the date.

## The part nobody talks about

The money is real. Cut three dead subscriptions at ₦3,000 each and that is ₦108,000 back over a year.

But the better part is not having it in your head. Right now you are quietly tracking renewal dates, which card is on file, whether that thing you cancelled actually cancelled, and who still owes you for Spotify. None of that is heavy on its own. It just never stops.

Let the app hold it.

## You can start today, even without an app

1. Open three months of bank statements and find every recurring debit. Write them all down.
2. Split them into three groups: use every week, use sometimes, have not opened in months.
3. Cancel the third group today. All of it. You will not miss any of them.
4. Note the renewal date and amount for whatever is left.
5. Set a reminder two days before each one.

Step 5 is where everybody gives up. That is the part software should be doing.
